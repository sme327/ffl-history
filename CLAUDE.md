The Long Game

Project Mission

The Long Game is a digital museum preserving the history of a fantasy football league that has existed continuously since 2001.

This is not a fantasy football analytics site.

This is not a Yahoo replacement.

This is not a statistics dashboard.

The purpose of the project is to preserve the memory of a 25-year community.

Championships, drafts, keepers, trades, standings, rivalries, and records are all artifacts that help tell the story.

The stories themselves are the product.

⸻

Core Design Philosophy

When building new features, prioritize:

1. Story
2. Legacy
3. Identity
4. Rivalry
5. Statistics

Statistics support stories.

Statistics should rarely be the final destination.

⸻

Desired User Experience

A league member should spend time exploring the site and repeatedly say:

“Oh wow, I forgot about that.”

Examples:

* A forgotten championship run
* A legendary keeper
* A painful playoff loss
* A franchise lineage
* A player owned across multiple eras
* An old team name
* A rivalry that defined a decade

The site succeeds when it creates nostalgia and rediscovery.

⸻

Visual Identity

The Long Game should feel like:

* A Hall of Fame
* A Sports Museum
* A League History Documentary
* A Trophy Room

The site should NOT feel like:

* Yahoo Fantasy
* ESPN Fantasy
* Sleeper
* A betting application
* A generic analytics dashboard

⸻

Page Philosophy

Every page should follow a similar structure:

Hero

↓

Story

↓

Records

↓

Deep Dive

↓

Raw Data

Raw data should support the story, not dominate it.

⸻

Page Ownership

Home

Question:

“What is this place?”

Purpose:

Introduce the league and invite exploration.

⸻

Champions

Question:

“Who won?”

Purpose:

Celebrate champions, dynasties, title runs, and championship heartbreak.

⸻

Timeline

Question:

“What happened?”

Purpose:

Serve as the historical spine of the entire site.

All major pages should eventually contribute notable events to Timeline.

Timeline is the league’s history book.

⸻

League History

Question:

“How did the league evolve?”

Purpose:

League eras, rule changes, scoring evolution, competitive balance, and historical trends.

⸻

Season Archive

Question:

“What happened in a specific year?”

Purpose:

Create documentary-style season histories.

Each season should feel like its own chapter in the league story.

Include NFL context when possible.

⸻

Managers

Question:

“Who are these people?”

Purpose:

Manager identities, personalities, accomplishments, failures, rivalries, and mythology.

Managers should feel like characters, not records.

⸻

Franchises

Question:

“What happened to this seat over time?”

Purpose:

Franchise lineage, stewardship, continuity, identity, and legacy.

Franchises are not managers.

Franchises are institutions.

⸻

Draft Center

Question:

“How were contenders built?”

Purpose:

Draft behavior, draft philosophy, player loyalty, and draft history.

⸻

Keeper Hall

Question:

“Who couldn’t let go?”

Purpose:

Keeper dynasties, keeper lore, keeper identity, and player attachment.

⸻

Rivalries

Question:

“Who still hates each other?”

Purpose:

The emotional center of competition.

Manager rivalries, franchise rivalries, playoff rivalries, championship rivalries, streaks, and heartbreak.

⸻

Narrative Layer Requirement

Every significant entity should eventually have a narrative summary.

Examples:

Managers

Franchises

Seasons

Players

Rivalries

Records

Not just:

Record: 184-142

But:

“One of the defining managers of the modern era, known for…”

⸻

Product Test

Before adding any feature, ask:

Does this help preserve the memory of the league?

If not, it probably does not belong.

If yes, determine whether it tells a story that cannot already be told elsewhere in the site.

The Long Game is a museum.

Always build toward that vision.
